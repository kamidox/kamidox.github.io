Self-introduction

Dear Sir/Madam,

This letter will introduce myself and also the milestones in my career. Hopefully, these stories can let you know me better.

Start the software engineer career¶

I graduated from Xiamen University in 2004 with a bachelor’s degree in automation. I join the R&D software team of Amoy Mobile after graduated. Amoy was a tier 1 Mobile Phone company at that time.

I worked as a software engineer at Amoy Mobile for three years. I learned a lot from this position and became a senior engineer after three years. I also found that I really enjoy working as a R&D software engineer.

The first milestone in my career was that I developed a prototype of a browser/MMS/email client that ran in an embedded feature phone. It took almost a year and has a total of 150,000 lines of code.

Why I was doing this? Here is the story.

When I was working at Amoy, I was responsible for the maintenance of the browser/MMS/email client. The software was purchased from a German company called OpenWave. We can’t see the core code, only can see some code at the system adapter layer.

I can’t see the code, which makes my work very inefficient. Due to the fact that there are always some protocol compatibility issues when the system is tested in the CMCC lab in China. The only solution is always to talk to OpenWave’s software engineers and ask them to solve the problem. It’s really inefficient.

One day, I wondered if it would be possible to develop a browser/MMS/email client on my own? After a long period of work, I was really familiar with the protocol.

And that’s where the whole story begins. So, I started writing code. Curiosity drove me to do it. My goal is to learn more from it and then make my day-to-day work easier: easy to communicate with OpenWave’s engineers and even tell them directly what the problem is.

I spent almost all of my spare time on this project. Finally, I made it. I implemented a prototype software that works on my phone. I learned a lot by doing this, and it helped a lot. Not only in my daily work, but also in my software development skills, I learned to do system architecture design, module design. In addition, I have gained a lot of experience in UI framework design and implementation, Internet protocols such as HTTP/TLS/IMAP/TCP/IP, and memory management.

When I look back on my career, I can clearly see that this side project has taken my technical skills to the next level. Typically, this type of software needs to be developed by a large team, not by an individual. This experience has helped me a lot in my career development. But at the time, I didn’t know. Just like Steve Jobs said: “You can’t connect the dots looking forward; you can only connect them looking backwards.”

From individual contributor to team leader¶

In 2007, I left Amoy and joined a small company, who develop mobile phone solutions. My new role was a team leader.

Not surprisingly, I learned a lot from my new role. You have to motivate the team, inspire the team. You have to do a lot of communication, a lot of feedback.

In addition to the change in role, another career milestone was my deep involvement in international R&D collaborations.

Here’s the story. In 2007, a big event happened in the mobile phone industry: the iPhone published. At that time, the entire industry was shifting from feature phones to smartphones. Qualcomm is the leading U.S. company in mobile phone solutions, they face a lot of challenges from Chinese companies. Because, Chinese companies offer turnkey solutions. This means that customers don’t need to put much effort into developing new smartphone products. Turnkey solutions already have a large number of drivers to match components such as cameras, LCDs, sensors, etc. In addition, it has a beautiful user interface.

That’s why Qualcomm asked us to develop a turnkey solution based on their technology platform. I was in charge of this project and needed to work with the Qualcomm US R&D team and the Qualcomm India R&D team. We have a meeting every morning. I report on the status of the project every day and ask for support from Qualcomm. We carefully review each issue and define the next actions. This has greatly improved my English and given me the opportunity to be deeply involved in international R&D collaborations. It has also improved my cross-cultural communication and collaboration skills.

A part of global inovation network¶

2015, I joined ABB. To develop the Door Entry System and Smart Home product and solutions. This is a new industry for me.

ABB is an established international organization with R&D centers all over the world. We have an open innovation center in Shenzhen, China, a technology development center in Xiamen, China, a product development center in Beijing, China, a technology development center focusing on KNX in Lüdenscheid, Sauerland, Germany, and another technology development center in Heidelberg, Germany, focusing on actuator device.

A typical project, the product manager is from Germany, the device R&D team is from China, the cloud R&D team is from Germany, most of the softwares are developed by the Chinese R&D team, but some software modules are developed by the German R&D team.

We work together as a virtual team to define product features and implementation timelines, conduct resource planning, develop technical solutions, and define quality control strategies.

The most challenging part is that the whole team has different backgrounds, comes from different countries, has different cultures, has different work styles, and comes from different time zones.

The key here is to plan well, communicate well, be transparent and straightforward. When possible, always try to develop personal relationships, which will also help in the execution of the project.

Future¶

A line in the movie “Forrest Gump” said: Life is like a box of chocolate. You never know what you’re going to get.

Always be passionate, always be curious, not only at work, but also in life.

I joined the “Machine Learning” course in coursera.org, which teach by Andrew Ng, a famous teacher from Stanford, and gain the certificate in 2015.

I wrote a book on topic “Scikit-learn Machine Learning” and published in 2017.

I love running. I’ve been running about 1000 km a year since 2015. It keeps me healthy, not only physically, but also mentally.

I like cycling. I ride 100km every weekend. And also encourage my older daughter (13 years old) to join me. She is now also enjoys cycling with me on weekends. It’s really interesting.

That’s who I am. Thanks for your time to read it.
